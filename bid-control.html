Quotax Bid Control

Stops thin-margin bids from going out without a named sign-off. One-page summary for internal routing.

The problem

Bids are built in a large spreadsheet that only one or two senior estimators fully understand. Margin gets shaved to win work, and nothing consistently flags a job that is too thin before it goes to the client. When a senior estimator retires, that judgement leaves with them.

What it does

1 · Floor per bid
The estimator or approver sets the margin floor on each bid, because the right floor depends on scope and risk.
2 · Thin-job flag
Internal cost and markup on every line. Below the floor, the bid is flagged with the dollar shortfall and the thin sections (e.g. framing & drywall, finishes).
3 · Hold at send
A bid under its floor cannot be printed or shared. It is held until a second person, not the estimator, signs off with a name and a reason.
4 · Approval record
Every release is time-stamped with who approved it and why. Costs, margins and approvals never appear on the client's copy.

What it is not

No plan take-off, no BIM, no logins, no implementation project. Estimators open a web page and type their lines. It sits alongside your existing estimating, at the last step before a bid leaves the building.

Suggested pilot (when there is an owner)

  1. Owner: one preconstruction lead who sets floors and acts as approver.
  2. Scope: 2 estimators run their next 10 live bids through Bid Control alongside the spreadsheet, for 30 days.
  3. Measure: how many bids were flagged thin, how many were released and why, and whether any thin bid would have gone out unnoticed.
  4. Cost: no charge for the pilot. Pricing is agreed only if the pilot shows it catches bids your current process misses.
